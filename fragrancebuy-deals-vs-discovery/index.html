CASE STUDY / ECOMMERCE + CRO

Deals vs.
Discovery.

Same store. Two very different reasons to shop.

I wanted to know whether a fragrance shopper responds more to finding a really good deal, or feeling confident they’ve found the right scent. So I designed two homepage directions to test that question.

01 / THE STARTING POINT

FragranceBuy already has a strong reason to shop there: the deal.

Its value proposition is pretty clear: authentic fragrances, a huge catalogue and warehouse-style pricing. That works beautifully when you already know which bottle you want.

But sometimes I know I want something warm, sweet, clean, sexy or summery… and I have absolutely no idea what that perfume is called. That tension is where this project started.

This is an independent exploration inspired by FragranceBuy’s live store ↗. It was not commissioned by FragranceBuy.

02 / THE QUESTION

What if I explored two different reasons to click?

A / DEAL-LED

“That price is too good to pass up.”

B / DISCOVERY

“Wait… I actually want that.”

03 / TWO DIRECTIONS

Two homepages.
Two hypotheses.

PROTOTYPE A / DEAL-LED

Make the value impossible to miss.

If savings, urgency and product access are front and centre, shoppers with buying intent may reach products and the cart faster.

DEAL → URGENCY → PRODUCT → CART
Look for: hero clicks · quick-add · add-to-cart · revenue per visitor
PROTOTYPE B / TRUST + DISCOVERY

Make choosing feel easier.

If shoppers can explore by scent and get answers to their questions early, they may browse deeper and feel more confident buying.

DISCOVER → LEARN → TRUST → PRODUCT
Look for: scent finder · education · reviews · conversion

04 / MAPPING THE EXPERIENCE

Designing the conversion logic before the UI.

Before getting into visual design, I mapped what every section needed to do: catch attention, create urgency, answer a question or move someone closer to the cart. Each page needed its own rhythm.

05 / DIRECTION A · DEAL-LED

What happens when I turn the deal all the way up?

I leaned into what FragranceBuy already does well: warehouse pricing, visible discounts and fast ways into product. This direction is deliberately louder.

01 / FIRST IMPRESSION

Make the value obvious immediately.

Authenticity, warehouse pricing and the discount are visible before the first scroll. There’s no mystery about why you’d shop here.

I’d expect more hero CTA clicks from shoppers ready to buy.

02 / DEALS + QUICK ADD

Shorten the path from “good deal” to cart.

What it is, what it costs, what you’re saving and how to buy it sit together. Shoppers can act without opening every product page.

I’d watch quick-add clicks and add-to-cart rate, then check whether that intent survives checkout.

03 / SHOPPING SHORTCUTS

Give different kinds of shoppers a way in.

“What the FragComm is buying” answers what’s popular. Shop by format helps someone go straight to boxed bottles, testers, gift sets or decants.

I’d track product clicks and products viewed per session by entry point.

04 / CART BUILDING

Make free shipping feel within reach.

Instead of keeping the threshold tucked away until checkout, a progress bar makes it tangible: “You’re $53.18 away.”

I’d test whether this lifts revenue per visitor without creating more cart abandonment.

06 / MEASURING A

If A works, I’d expect faster commercial behaviour.

The core signals are hero CTA click-through, product and quick-add clicks, add-to-cart and revenue per visitor. I’d also watch cart abandonment so an easier add doesn’t just move friction downstream.

My expectation: A may work harder for shoppers who already know what they want.

07 / DIRECTION B · TRUST + DISCOVERY

What if I make choosing the fragrance easier instead?

Sometimes the sticking point isn’t price. It’s “Will I like it? Is it real? What’s a tester?” This direction tries to answer those questions before asking for a purchase.

01 / SCENT FINDER

Start with what the shopper knows.

Not everyone knows a fragrance name. They might know they want fresh and clean, or warm and spicy. So discovery starts in language people actually use.

I’d look for scent finder and quiz starts, then follow those sessions into products.

02 / AUTHENTICITY + FORMATS

Answer the slightly awkward questions early.

Why is it cheaper? Is it real? What’s a tester? I made those answers part of the shopping journey instead of hiding them in a help page.

I’d compare engagement with these explanations against downstream product and cart behaviour.

03 / BEST BLIND BUYS

Make a scent easier to imagine.

Warm, fresh, floral and sweet cues give each product a little more meaning. They help bridge “I’ve never smelled this” and “Actually… this sounds like me.”

I’d measure product discovery clicks, PDP visits and add-to-cart from this module.

04 / EDUCATION + REASSURANCE

Confidence comes from more than one place.

Some shoppers need fragrance basics. Others need reviews, authenticity proof or a clear return policy. B layers those signals throughout the journey.

I’d watch education, review and FAQ engagement alongside eventual conversion.

Curious which scent you’d find?

08 / MEASURING B

B should tell a different story in the data.

Scent Finder clicks and quiz starts tell me if the new entry point is useful. Education, reviews and FAQs tell me whether shoppers use the reassurance. Add-to-cart and conversion tell me whether any of it actually helps people buy.

My expectation: B may create deeper engagement for people who are still figuring out what they want. Bounce rate is a guardrail for shoppers who simply want a deal.

Same catalogue. Different shopping psychology.

10 / IF I COULD SHIP THIS

I wouldn’t ask which looks better. I’d test what actually creates value.

TEST 01

Current site vs. A

Does amplifying the existing deal behaviour help high-intent shoppers move to product and cart more effectively?

TEST 02

Current site vs. B

Does guided discovery and visible reassurance help browsing-led shoppers choose and purchase?

THEN SEGMENT

Who needs which?

Look at acquisition source, new vs. returning shoppers and whether someone arrives with a specific product in mind. Set a primary outcome and sample size before running each test.

These are proposed experiments. Neither concept was run against live traffic, so this project does not claim measured conversion lifts.

11 / WHAT I LEARNED

Sometimes CRO isn’t about making one button perform better.

This started as a homepage redesign and got much more interesting once I asked, “Why is this person here in the first place?” The same person might want a screaming deal when they know the fragrance, and a little guidance when they don’t.

So the question I’d really want to test is: when does a shopper need each one?

“I already know what I want. Help me find the best deal and get to the product quickly.” “I’m not completely sure what I want. Help me explore my options and feel confident in my choice.”

Prototype A Deal-Led Warehouse Sale The experience immediately emphasizes discounts, product availability and quick access to popular categories. Search is highly visible, promotional messaging is direct and customers can begin browsing products with minimal friction. The hypothesis If most visitors already understand fragrance and arrive with purchase intent, a deal-led homepage should help them find products faster and move toward checkout with fewer distractions. Prototype B Trust and Discovery Instead of leading primarily with discounts, the homepage helps customers understand the products, explore scent preferences and feel more comfortable purchasing fragrance online. The hypothesis If uncertainty is preventing customers from purchasing, a homepage that provides guidance and reassurance should increase engagement and improve conversion among new visitors.

Key design decisions Prominent search Clear warehouse-sale messaging Deal-focused product collections Product-type shortcuts Visible savings Key design decisions Trust-led introduction Find Your Scent navigation Scent Finder Beginner-friendly recommendations Product education

Prototype A vs. Prototype B The two concepts are intentionally different because they are designed to answer a strategic question.

Prototype A asks: Can faster access to deals and products increase purchase activity? Prototype B asks: Can guidance and reassurance help more customers feel confident enough to buy?

Proposed A/B Testing Strategy

This measures whether each experience contributes to meaningful business results rather than only generating clicks.

Additional Metrics to Track

How I Would Interpret the Results?

If Prototype A performs better Customers may already arrive with strong purchase intent and mainly need efficient access to products, prices and promotions. The homepage should continue prioritizing search, deals and fast product discovery. If Prototype B performs better Customers may require more reassurance and guidance before purchasing fragrance online. The homepage should invest further in education, personalization and trust-building tools.

If the results are mixed...

The strongest solution may combine the most effective elements from both concepts—for example, maintaining a deal-led hero while introducing discovery tools further down the page.

Final Takeaway

The purpose of this project was not to choose between deals and discovery based on personal preference. It was to create two testable concepts and outline how customer behaviour could be used to evaluate them. Neither design direction was tested with live traffic. Good ecommerce design does not assume what customers need. It creates a clear hypothesis, measures the response and continues improving from there.
